# Check 02 — too-clean narrator

Universal method, stripped from `max-human-mess-narrator-pass`. Runs only on **first-person
reflection** (personal essay, reflective Substack/newsletter). Catches a narrator who is too
reconciled, too self-knowing, or too convenient: he knows what the experience meant before it is
over, and he is right about himself the whole time. Clean characterization is the AI-tidy tell
applied to a person.

Proposes findings only. Renders no verdict. May propose zero findings.

## Applicability

Run only if the piece is first-person reflection. On non-reflective posts, emails, or
instructional pieces, this check is `Skipped` (record it, do not manufacture narrator findings).

## Reads from

- The measurement record (§2 section map).
- The supplied profile, if present: §Preserve list (so a deliberately unreconciled narrator is
  not mistaken for a failure), §Native pressures.

## Method

1. Identify the narrator's apparent self-understanding: what does he think is happening, and does
   he already know what it means?
2. The three base categories:
   - **Mixed motives** — is the narrator acting from one clean motive, or several at once?
   - **Bodily pressure** — is the body present under pressure, or does the narrator move through
     events as pure mind?
   - **Inevitable-not-random** — if mess is present, does it read as "of course" or as decoration?
3. The four narrator-specific failures:
   - **Too-smooth self-understanding** — does he already know what the experience meant?
   - **Contradiction named instead of enacted** — does he report mixed feelings, or does his
     behavior contain them?
   - **Right about himself throughout** — does relief stay only relief, anger only justified?
     When a beat lands cleanly, ask what it is also.
   - **Essay-function behavior** — does he exist only to carry an insight from open to close,
     arriving exactly when the piece needs him, never misreading the situation?
4. Distinguish human mess from random quirk. A detail is mess if it would make the reader say "of
   course." It is noise if it has no emotional necessity. Do not reward invented quirk.
5. Find the single smallest-change repair target. Prefer one crack in the narrator's self-
   certainty over global revision.
6. Note what the piece is already doing well and must not lose (body imagery, concrete pressure,
   strong existing lines).

## Output

Proposed findings (scorecard rows) + a Protect list + a "do not add" note (what a revision must
not introduce). For each: Check `Narrator: <failure type>`, Severity, Evidence quote (the exact
beat), Source rule (`checks/02-narrator-clean.md §<failure>`), What's off, Suggested fix direction
(the single smallest change that opens the narrator up).

If no narrator failure is present, report "no narrator findings from this check."

## Hard rules

- This is not "make prose messier."
- Do not add random quirks or invented contradiction.
- Do not add therapy-speak or explicit self-psychologizing.
- Do not push the piece toward confession or overexpose the writer.
- Must preserve body imagery, concrete pressure, and strong existing lines.
- Prefer one targeted repair over global revision.
- Never import the writer's private biography to "deepen" the narrator. The gate works from the
  text, not from a dossier.
