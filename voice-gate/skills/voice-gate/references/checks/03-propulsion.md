# Check 03 — propulsion (the one piece-level lens)

Universal method, stripped from `max-pressure-propulsion-pass`. This is the single piece-level
lens in the gate; everything else is line altitude. It asks whether the piece **compounds** or
merely **inventories**. A locally lovely piece can be globally inert: warm sentences, the same
realization re-run at the same intensity. A reflection compounds; an inventory repeats.

Proposes findings only. Renders no verdict. May propose zero findings (and report "compounds").

## Reads from

- The measurement record (§2 section map).
- The supplied profile, if present: §Native pressures (which of the nine this writer's strongest
  work runs on, and which are off-voice or overused).

## Method

1. **Name the live question.** What is the reader being asked to hold across the whole piece? If
   no live question is identifiable, that is the primary finding.
2. **Name the pressure actually present.** The nine-pressure taxonomy: danger, desire, secret,
   shame, contradiction, decision, dread, curiosity, longing. Do not assign pressure from the
   list; use it to diagnose what is already there or what is absent. If a profile is supplied and
   names the writer's native pressures, flag a piece running on only one when the writer's range
   is wider ("working from a third of the engine"). Without a profile, diagnose presence/absence
   of live pressure but do not claim the writer is underusing their range.
3. **Run the compounding test, section by section** (from the section map):
   - Does each section raise the cost of the live question, or restate the same insight at the
     same intensity?
   - Could a later beat be deleted without changing what an earlier beat meant? If yes, it is
     restatement, not compounding.
   - Where does the draft reset instead of compound?
4. **Distinguish good friction from bad friction.**
   - **Good friction — protect it:** moral discomfort, delayed legibility, unresolved
     contradiction, tonal abrasion, a narrator not fully legible to himself. Removing good
     friction in the name of clarity produces a cleaner, emptier piece.
   - **Bad friction — flag it:** confusion, repetition, inert abstraction, a section making the
     same point again without raising the cost.
5. For bad friction only: name the smallest-change target. Do not recommend adding pressure from
   the taxonomy; recommend returning to the material for what is being avoided.

## Output

- **Live question:** what the reader is asked to hold.
- **Pressure present:** which taxonomy pressure(s) are active.
- **Section map:** each section tagged pressure-active / restatement / cost-rises.
- Proposed findings (bad friction only; scorecard rows): Check `Propulsion: <restatement / inert
  abstraction / reset / no live question / single-pressure>`, Severity, Evidence quote, Source
  rule (`checks/03-propulsion.md §The compounding test` or `profile §Native pressures`), What's
  off, Suggested fix direction.
- **Good friction to protect:** passages that must survive.

If the piece compounds and good friction is present, report "Propulsion: compounds; good friction
present" as a Pass finding. It is a finding, not a verdict.

## Hard rules

- Do not turn every piece into a dramatic arc.
- Do not assign pressure from the taxonomy; diagnose what is present or absent.
- Do not sand away productive discomfort, unresolved contradiction, or tonal abrasion.
- Do not confuse clarity with aliveness.
- Must be allowed to report "compounds" and propose no fixes; must not render a readiness verdict.
