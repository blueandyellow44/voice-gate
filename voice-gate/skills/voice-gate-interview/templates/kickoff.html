voice-gate kickoff · build a supplied voice profile by interview

Answer in your own words. Anything you only assert is marked self-reported until a sample or the calibration gate proves it. The gate at the end is what makes the profile trustworthy.

1 · Identity and register map

When you write with no one to impress, how do you sound? Type one plain line.
How does that voice shift per form? Give an opening line you would actually write in each.
Personal material: how much reaches the page in an essay vs a work email?
Sets the interior dosing ladder: high in essay, low in newsletter, near-zero in email.

2 · Hard guardrails (mechanical)

Punctuation you never use or always use? (em dashes, semicolons, Oxford comma, exclamation points)
Contractions: everywhere, never in composed work, or only casual?
Words, openings, or sign-offs you refuse or always use?
A rule you cannot actually keep is not a guardrail. It stays self-reported until proven.

3 · Figurative signature

Do you reach for figures (metaphor, simile, images), or write plain and concrete?
Which figurative moves feel not-you?
Type the most figurative sentence you have written recently.
"Near-zero figuration, plain register" is a valid, honest answer.

4 · Native pressures

Which two or three does your strongest writing run on?
Which are off-voice or overused for you?
Name a piece that worked. What was pulling under it?
The pressure and the piece, not a psychological story.

5 · Preserve list / signature moves

What do editors try to "fix" that is actually you on purpose?
When something is hard, do you name the feeling or let it sit under the details? Show how.
Sets the silence rule: interior that steers without being stated.

6 · Known surface tells (optional)

When your writing is bad, what does it do? Your specific tell.

7 · Samples (optional)

Paste real writing, or load a .txt / .md file. Samples outrank your answers: if the prose contradicts a claim, the prose wins.
No samples is fine. Slots stay self-reported and the calibration gate carries the grounding.
